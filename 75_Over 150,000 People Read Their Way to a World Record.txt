On November 4, Guinness World Records adjudicator Will Munford walked into Malmesbury Primary School in London with a certificate for a reading party that had taken place a month earlier. On October 8, 153,862 people in 92 countries and territories logged on for Take 10 to Read, an event run by the National Literacy Trust and the British Council for Britain's National Year of Reading 2026. Many joined a short online session with a guest, then spent about ten minutes with a book, a comic, or an audiobook they had chosen. Guinness counted those readers across a 12-hour span and, on November 5, published the title: the most people reading online in 12 hours.

More than 3,000 schools, libraries, families, and other groups took part. Outside Britain, the largest groups were in Romania, India, and Pakistan. Take 10 to Read began in 2018 as a local pause of ten minutes; this year, with the British Council, it went global. At the London school, children who had joined the October event could see the certificate. Children's writer Rashmi Sirdeshpande, who had led one of the sessions, read with them and answered questions. A similar presentation took place at Holy Child Senior Secondary School in New Delhi.

"From classrooms and libraries to homes across the world, 153,862 people in 92 countries each played their part," Munford said. "You don't have to do something extraordinary on your own to achieve something extraordinary together." David Hayman, who directs the National Year of Reading, called it a reminder of what happens when people gather around the same love of reading: it can bring them together, open new worlds, and support everyday wellbeing. For one stretch of an October day, the screens that often steal an evening opened onto a shared start, a time to begin a page while thousands of other rooms did the same.
