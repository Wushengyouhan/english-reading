On November 9 in London, 81-year-old M. John Harrison walked onto a stage with a 198-page novel and left with the Booker Prize: 50,000 pounds and a trophy handed to him by last year's winner. He had been publishing for 60 years, and The End of Everything was his 13th novel, his first time on the Booker list, and the shortest of the six books still in the race. The judges had read 163 novels before choosing it. At 81 he is now the oldest Booker winner, two years older than Margaret Atwood was when she shared the prize in 2019.

The book is a satirical story set on England's south coast, a few decades from now. A beachcomber and his elderly aunt try to keep a life going after a disaster no one can fully explain. Strange visitors have arrived, ordinary days still happen at the seaside, and the old rules no longer hold. Mary Beard, who chaired the judges, called it "the politics of now seen through the lens of the future." After a full day of debate, the panel chose it unanimously. Beard said the novel "turns you upside down, raises a wry smile" and, despite its dark material, "leaves you with a twinge of optimism."

Harrison has spent those 60 years moving between science fiction, fantasy, and literary fiction, as a novelist and as a critic. In 2022 he sat on the other side of the table, as a Booker judge. This year the prize came to him. He has said current affairs gave him the satire, and a question that sits under the book: what happens when all our explanations of the world become a disaster, but we still have to make a life?

Late in a career, he told an interviewer, you write to see how far you can go, because this might be the last book. The Booker arrived on that slower clock. After 13 novels and six decades, the shortest book in the room was the one that won.
