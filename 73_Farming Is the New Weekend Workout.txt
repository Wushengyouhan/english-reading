Xu Min, a university professor, and her husband Hou Yucheng, a researcher, spend their days surrounded by academic papers and data. But on weekends, they trade their keyboards for shovels.

This year, the couple discovered an unusual form of stress relief: becoming "garden owners" at the Taiyuan Botanical Garden in Shanxi province. Despite living far away, their 17-square-meter plot has become a "spiritual sanctuary" for them and their 2-year-old daughter.

"Being immersed in nature, my whole mood lifts," Xu said. Her husband agrees, noting that working in the garden fully relieves their mental and physical fatigue.

Across Chinese cities, leasing land in the suburbs to grow vegetables is becoming increasingly popular. The Taiyuan Botanical Garden launched its rental program in 2022. Renters get yearlong land use rights and keep all the vegetables they harvest.

Participants can choose a self-planting zone, where they do all the work, or a managed zone, where professional farmers handle the cultivation. "The number of participants grows every year. This year, more than 160 households have signed up," said a botanical garden staff member. Tools, planting guidance, and water are all provided.

For many urbanites, growing vegetables represents a shift in lifestyle. They are no longer satisfied with shopping or gym workouts; they want something more "grounded."

Zheng Lili, an office worker seeking an escape from her monotonous routine, rented a managed plot. "Gardening replaces mental fatigue with physical exhaustion," she said. "When I'm working the soil, I don't think about work problems. I can completely quiet my mind."

Zhao Yu, a young mother, brings her daughter to help out. "The sense of accomplishment is enormous," she said, adding that gardening is so physically demanding it has completely replaced her gym workouts.

Jiang Jianbo, a local research institute director, notes this trend is more than just an agricultural activity. "It's an emotional connection deeply rooted in our cultural bloodline," he said. By tending their gardens, people build emotional bridges to their ancestors and reconnect with traditional agricultural culture.
